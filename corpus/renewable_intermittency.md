# Intermittency of Renewables

Wind and solar are variable renewable energy sources: their output depends on weather and time of day, not on demand. As their share of electricity grows, matching supply with demand becomes harder.

The main tools for managing intermittency are storage (batteries for hours, pumped hydro and other long-duration options for days), flexible demand, stronger transmission connecting different weather regions, overbuilding renewable capacity, and firm low-carbon sources such as hydro, geothermal and nuclear.

Studies of high-renewable grids generally find that combining several of these tools is cheaper than relying on any single one. Wind and solar also complement each other, because winds are often stronger in winter and at night while solar peaks in summer and at midday.
