# Geothermal Energy

Geothermal energy uses heat from inside the Earth. Conventional geothermal plants tap naturally hot water or steam reservoirs, which exist in volcanic regions such as Iceland, Kenya, Indonesia and parts of the United States.

Geothermal power is firm: it runs around the clock regardless of weather, which makes it valuable alongside wind and solar. Its main limitation has been geography, because high-quality reservoirs are rare.

Enhanced geothermal systems (EGS) aim to remove that limit by drilling deep into hot dry rock and creating fractures for water to circulate, borrowing drilling techniques from the oil and gas industry. Ground-source heat pumps are a different, shallow use of geothermal heat for heating buildings.
