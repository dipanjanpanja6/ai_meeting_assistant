# Nuclear Power

Nuclear power plants generate heat from fission, the splitting of uranium atoms, and use it to make steam that drives turbines. They produce very low-carbon electricity and run at high capacity factors, often above 90 percent.

Large new reactors in Europe and North America have suffered major cost overruns and delays, which has made new nuclear expensive compared with wind and solar. Long-term storage of spent fuel remains a political challenge, although the volume of waste is small.

Small modular reactors (SMRs) aim to cut costs by building standardised reactors in factories. Supporters argue they could provide firm, low-carbon power that complements variable renewables. Critics note that few SMRs are operating and that their cost advantage is not yet proven.
