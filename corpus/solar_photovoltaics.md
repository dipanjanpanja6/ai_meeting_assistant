# Solar Photovoltaics

Solar photovoltaic (PV) panels turn sunlight directly into electricity using semiconductor cells, most commonly made of crystalline silicon. When photons hit the cell they free electrons, creating a direct current that an inverter converts to alternating current for the grid.

The cost of solar modules has fallen by roughly 90 percent since 2010, driven by manufacturing scale, thinner wafers and higher cell efficiencies. Commercial silicon panels now typically convert 20 to 23 percent of incoming sunlight into electricity.

Solar output is variable: it peaks at midday, drops on cloudy days and is zero at night. This creates the so-called "duck curve", where net demand on the grid falls sharply at midday and ramps up steeply in the evening. Pairing solar with batteries is the most common way to shift midday energy into the evening peak.
