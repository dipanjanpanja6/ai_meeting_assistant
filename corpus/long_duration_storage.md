# Long-Duration Energy Storage

Long-duration energy storage (LDES) refers to technologies that can discharge for ten hours or more. They are needed to cover multi-day wind droughts and seasonal gaps that short-duration lithium-ion batteries cannot economically fill.

Pumped hydro storage is the oldest and largest form: water is pumped uphill when power is cheap and released through turbines when it is needed. It accounts for the vast majority of the world's installed storage capacity, but it requires suitable geography.

Newer options include flow batteries, which store energy in liquid electrolytes in external tanks; iron-air batteries, which use the rusting and un-rusting of iron; compressed air storage in underground caverns; and thermal storage in hot sand, rock or molten salt. Most are still early in commercial deployment, and their costs are uncertain.
