# Carbon Capture and Storage

Carbon capture and storage (CCS) separates carbon dioxide from industrial exhaust or power plant flue gas, compresses it, and injects it deep underground into rock formations for permanent storage.

CCS is mainly discussed for sectors with process emissions that are hard to avoid, such as cement, where carbon dioxide is released by the chemical reaction itself, not only by burning fuel. Many projects have captured less carbon than planned, and costs remain high.

Direct air capture (DAC) removes carbon dioxide from ambient air rather than from a concentrated exhaust stream. Because air contains only about 0.04 percent carbon dioxide, DAC needs much more energy per tonne and is currently far more expensive than point-source capture.
