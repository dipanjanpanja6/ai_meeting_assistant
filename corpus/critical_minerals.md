# Critical Minerals

Clean energy technologies require more minerals than fossil fuel systems. Batteries need lithium, nickel, cobalt, manganese and graphite; wind turbines and EV motors use rare earth elements for permanent magnets; and grids need large amounts of copper.

Supply chains are concentrated. A large share of cobalt is mined in the Democratic Republic of Congo, and refining of lithium, graphite and rare earths is dominated by China. This concentration creates price volatility and geopolitical risk.

Responses include diversifying mining and refining, recycling batteries at end of life, and changing chemistries. The shift toward lithium iron phosphate batteries, for example, removes the need for cobalt and nickel. Mining also raises environmental and human rights concerns that must be managed.
