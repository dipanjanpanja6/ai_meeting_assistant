# The Electricity Grid

The electricity grid must balance supply and demand every second. If they drift apart, the grid frequency moves away from its target of 50 or 60 hertz, and large deviations can trigger blackouts.

Traditional power plants with heavy spinning turbines provide inertia, which naturally slows frequency changes. Solar panels and batteries connect through power electronics and do not provide inertia by default. Grid-forming inverters, synchronous condensers and fast frequency response from batteries are used to replace it.

Transmission is a major bottleneck. Many wind and solar projects wait years in interconnection queues because new high-voltage lines take a long time to plan, permit and build. Expanding transmission lets regions share surplus renewable power and smooth out local weather.
