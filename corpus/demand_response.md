# Demand Response and Flexibility

Demand response means changing when electricity is used rather than how much is generated. Customers are paid or given cheaper tariffs to reduce or shift their consumption when the grid is stressed.

Examples include industrial plants pausing energy-intensive processes, smart thermostats pre-heating or pre-cooling homes, water heaters running at night, and electric vehicles charging when wind output is high. Time-of-use tariffs, which make power cheaper at off-peak hours, are a simple form of demand response.

Flexibility from demand can be cheaper than building new power plants or storage to meet rare peaks. Aggregators combine thousands of small devices into a "virtual power plant" that can be dispatched like a single generator.
