# Wind Power

Wind turbines convert the kinetic energy of moving air into electricity. The power available in the wind rises with the cube of wind speed, so a site with winds twice as fast offers about eight times the energy. This is why turbines have grown taller: wind is faster and steadier higher above the ground.

Onshore wind is one of the cheapest sources of new electricity in many countries. Offshore wind has stronger and more consistent winds but higher installation and maintenance costs, because foundations, cables and vessels are expensive.

Wind output varies with the weather and can drop for days during calm high-pressure systems, sometimes called "wind droughts". Grid operators manage this with forecasting, geographic spread of wind farms, interconnectors and backup capacity.
