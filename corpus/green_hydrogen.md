# Green Hydrogen

Green hydrogen is produced by electrolysis: an electric current splits water into hydrogen and oxygen, powered by renewable electricity. "Grey" hydrogen, which is most hydrogen produced today, comes from natural gas and releases carbon dioxide.

Electrolysis loses roughly 30 percent of the input energy, and converting hydrogen back into electricity loses more. Because of these losses, using hydrogen to power cars or heat homes is usually less efficient than using electricity directly in batteries or heat pumps.

Hydrogen is most promising where direct electrification is hard: making fertiliser (ammonia), refining, producing low-carbon steel, and possibly shipping fuels. The main barriers are the high cost of electrolysers, the need for cheap renewable power, and the difficulty of storing and transporting a very light gas.
