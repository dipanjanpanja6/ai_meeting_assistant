# Lithium-Ion Batteries

Lithium-ion batteries store energy by moving lithium ions between a graphite anode and a metal-oxide cathode. They dominate electric vehicles and grid storage because of their high energy density and round-trip efficiency of around 85 to 95 percent.

Two cathode families lead the market. Nickel-manganese-cobalt (NMC) cells offer higher energy density, which suits long-range cars. Lithium iron phosphate (LFP) cells are cheaper, safer and last more cycles, so they are increasingly used for grid storage and standard-range vehicles.

Battery pack prices fell by about 90 percent between 2010 and the early 2020s. Most grid batteries today provide two to four hours of storage, which is ideal for shifting solar energy into the evening but not for covering multi-day shortfalls.
