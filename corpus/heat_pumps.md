# Heat Pumps

A heat pump moves heat rather than creating it. Using a refrigerant cycle, it extracts heat from outside air, the ground or water and releases it inside a building. Running in reverse, the same machine provides cooling.

Because it moves heat, a heat pump typically delivers three to four units of heat for every unit of electricity it uses. This ratio is called the coefficient of performance (COP). A gas boiler, by comparison, delivers slightly less than one unit of heat per unit of fuel.

Modern cold-climate air-source heat pumps work well below freezing, although their efficiency falls as outdoor temperatures drop. The main barriers to adoption are upfront cost, the need for well-insulated buildings and suitable radiators, and a shortage of trained installers.
