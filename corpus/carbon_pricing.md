# Carbon Pricing and Energy Policy

Carbon pricing puts a cost on greenhouse gas emissions so that polluters pay for the damage they cause. It takes two main forms: a carbon tax, which sets the price directly, and an emissions trading system (cap and trade), which caps total emissions and lets the market set the price of permits.

The European Union Emissions Trading System, launched in 2005, is the largest example. Higher carbon prices in recent years helped push coal out of the European power sector.

Many governments combine carbon pricing with subsidies, tax credits and regulations, such as renewable energy targets and vehicle emission standards. Critics argue carbon prices can be regressive, which is why some schemes return revenue to households as dividends.
