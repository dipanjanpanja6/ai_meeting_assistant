# Levelized Cost of Energy

The levelized cost of energy (LCOE) is the average cost per unit of electricity over a power plant's lifetime, including construction, financing, fuel, operation and maintenance. It is widely used to compare generation technologies.

By LCOE, new utility-scale solar and onshore wind are now among the cheapest sources of electricity in most of the world, cheaper than new coal or gas plants.

LCOE has limits. It does not capture when the electricity is produced or the cost of integrating variable sources into the grid, such as storage, backup and transmission. Analysts therefore also use measures like "system cost" or "value-adjusted LCOE" that account for timing and reliability.
